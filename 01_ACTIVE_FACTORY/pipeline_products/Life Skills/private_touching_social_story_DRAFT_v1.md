## RESEARCH BASIS (sources used)

1. **Vanderbilt Kennedy Center - Healthy Bodies toolkit**
   - "Punishing, shaming, or giving the behavior a lot of negative
     attention may actually make it happen more"
   - Teach WHERE and WHEN the behavior is allowed, not that it is bad
   - Use visuals: list of public vs private places + behaviors

2. **Middletown Autism Centre - teenage sexuality resources**
   - Private places = bedroom with door/curtains closed, toilet with
     door closed, shower with bathroom door closed
   - "Private" sign on a school room does NOT mean it is suitable
   - Replacement: do other things with hands (fidgets, toys, ask break)

3. **Vanderbilt/McLaughlin - Elevatus Training curriculum**
   - Nonjudgmental framing: "it is okay to explore private parts in a
     private place such as bedroom or bathroom at home"
   - Public vs private is a discrimination skill - teach explicitly

4. **CSUMB thesis (2018) - Social stories targeting inappropriate
   sexual behavior in adolescents with autism**
   - Social stories successfully decreased inappropriate genital
   touching in two adolescent participants
   - Functional relationship demonstrated with no overlapping data

5. **Carol Gray social story guidelines**
   - Descriptive sentences > directive sentences (ratio ~2:1)
   - Calm, non-shaming tone
   - One concept per page
   - Visual support on every page

## SOCIAL STORY CONTENT (12 pages)

### Page 1: Introduction
**Text:**
"My body is growing up. Some parts of my body are private parts."

**Icon:** `body_changes_normal.png`
**Notes:** Neutral, positive opening. Establishes body growth as normal.

---

### Page 2: What are private parts?
**Text:**
"Private parts are the parts of my body that I cover with my underwear
or my swimsuit. These parts are just for me."

**Icon:** PLACEHOLDER `body_diagram_private.png` (simple body outline
with private areas shaded - NOT anatomical, just a covered-body silhouette)
**Notes:** Concrete definition. "Underwear or swimsuit" is the standard
SPED teaching frame used by Vanderbilt and Autism Speaks.

---

### Page 3: Touching my own body is OK
**Text:**
"It is okay to touch my own private parts. Touching my own body is
not bad. It is a normal thing that many people do."

**Icon:** `body_changes_normal.png` or PLACEHOLDER
**Notes:** CRITICAL non-shaming page. Research is explicit: shaming
increases the behavior and damages future help-seeking. This page
must feel calm and accepting.

---

### Page 4: But only in private places
**Text:**
"I can touch my private parts in private places. I cannot touch my
private parts in public places."

**Icon:** `private.png`
**Notes:** This is the key discrimination. Introduces public vs private
PLACES (not behaviors yet).

---

### Page 5: What is a private place?
**Text:**
"Private places are places where I am alone and the door is closed.
My bedroom with the door closed is a private place. The bathroom with
the door closed is a private place."

**Icon:** PLACEHOLDER `bedroom.png` + `restroom.png` (side by side)
**Notes:** Concrete examples. Middletown Autism: bedroom + bathroom
are the two universally agreed private places.

---

### Page 6: What is a public place?
**Text:**
"Public places are places where other people can see me. My classroom
is a public place. The bus is a public place. The cafeteria is a
public place. The store is a public place."

**Icon:** PLACEHOLDER `classroom.png` + `bus_stop.png` + `cafeteria.png`
**Notes:** Concrete contrast. Use familiar school/community locations.

---

### Page 7: If I am in public
**Text:**
"If I am in a public place and I want to touch my private parts, I
wait. I keep my hands on top of my clothes. I can hold something in
my hands instead."

**Icon:** PLACEHOLDER `hands_on_top.png` + `play.png`
**Notes:** Replacement strategy page. "Hold something" = fidget, pencil,
ball. Concrete alternative to the behavior.

---

### Page 8: What I can do with my hands
**Text:**
"If my hands want to go to my private parts, I can:
- Put my hands on my desk
- Hold a fidget or a ball
- Ask for a break
- Go to the bathroom"

**Icon:** `break.png` + `help.png` + `restroom.png` (choice array)
**Notes:** This page pairs with the Choice Cards component (#6).
Four concrete replacement actions.

---

### Page 9: Going to a private place
**Text:**
"If I need to touch my private parts, I can go to a private place.
I walk to the bathroom. I close the door. Now I am in a private place."

**Icon:** `restroom.png` + PLACEHOLDER `door_closed.png`
**Notes:** Action sequence. Pairs with Routine Strip component (#7).

---

### Page 10: Trusted adults can help
**Text:**
"If I am not sure, I can tell a trusted adult. My teacher, my parent,
or my caregiver can help me. They will not be angry. They want to help
me keep my body private."

**Icon:** `staff.png` + `caregiver.png` + `help.png`
**Notes:** Reduces shame-based secrecy. Research: autistic youth have
high sexual victimization rates - trusted adult access is protective.

---

### Page 11: Growing up
**Text:**
"Keeping my body private is part of growing up. I am learning to
take care of my body. I can do this."

**Icon:** `body_changes_normal.png` + `thumbs_up.png` (aac_core)
**Notes:** Positive close. Affirming, not punitive. Emphasizes growth
and capability.

---

### Page 12: My reminder
**Text:**
"My hands stay on top in public. My private parts stay private in
public. I go to a private place if I need to."

**Icon:** `private.png` + `stop.png`
**Notes:** Summary/reminder page. Can be printed as a wall visual.
Short, memorable, repeatable.

## TEACHER/PARENT GUIDE (2 pages)

### Page A: How to use this resource
- Read this story with the learner at a calm time, NOT right after
  an incident
- Read it regularly (daily at first, then weekly) so the language
  becomes familiar
- Use a calm, neutral tone - this is not a punishment, it is
  learning about growing up
- Let the learner hold a fidget while reading
- Stop and ask comprehension questions (included in this pack)
- Print the reminder page (page 12) and place it near the bathroom
  or in the learner's folder

### Page B: Important notes for adults
- This behavior is NORMAL. The goal is teaching WHERE and WHEN, not
  stopping the behavior entirely
- Shaming, punishing, or giving excessive negative attention can
  INCREASE the behavior and reduce future help-seeking
- Be proactive - introduce this story BEFORE the behavior becomes a
  problem, not only after
- If the behavior is very frequent or happens in many settings, talk
  to your BCBA or occupational therapist about a behavior plan
- Use correct anatomical terms (not slang) when answering questions
- The learner may have questions - answer them simply and honestly
- If you suspect abuse or victimization, contact your local child
  protection services - this resource does not replace professional
  intervention

## PARENT HANDOUT (1 page)

### Talking to your teen about private touching at home
- Your teen's body is changing. Touching private parts is normal.
- The goal is teaching WHERE and WHEN, not stopping the behavior.
- Use the same words at home that the school uses (consistency helps).
- Private places at home: bedroom with door closed, bathroom with
  door closed.
- If it happens in public at home (living room, kitchen), calmly say:
  "That is a private behavior. You can do that in your bedroom or the
  bathroom. Let's go there now."
- Do not shame, yell, or punish. Redirect calmly to a private place.
- If the behavior is very frequent, talk to your doctor or BCBA.
- Use correct body part names (not slang).
- Your teen can learn this. It takes time and repetition.
